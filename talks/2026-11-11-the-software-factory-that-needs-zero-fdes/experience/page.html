The software factory that needs zero FDEs

Raz Berman Gold argues that automated software factories should not require Forward Deployed Engineers to wire them in—connect your existing repos, models, and trackers behind scoped agents, deterministic pause gates, and scored retrospective memory.

Raz Berman Gold, Overcut · 20 min · Links and further reading

The premise

The FDE paradox and bring-your-own stack

Enterprise teams buying autonomous software-factory products keep hitting an ironic bottleneck: the platform shows up with Forward Deployed Engineers who take months to integrate it before the customer sees value:

“there is something strange about the fact that. You need to buy a product that automate your. Workflows, software development, but actually you need a human to integrate.”00:00:19

Berman Gold’s alternative plugs directly into tools teams already operate—GitHub, Jira, Bitbucket, Slack, existing repository skills, and any LLM provider or harness—deployable on isolated on-prem infrastructure, private cloud, or SaaS with no vendor lock-in:

“You can read any model. You can reach any tool, any harness. So we know how to get your existing tools and skills, and agents that you already built.”00:00:56

Workflows

Specialized agents, deterministic steps, and pause gates

Instead of giving one generalist agent access to every secret and tool, each agent has its own identity, narrow mission, and exact MCPs and skills—yielding higher quality at lower latency and token spend:

“You don't need an agent to do a lot of different missions. Any agent who has the access and the permission that it needs, invests this way.”00:02:42

Playbooks for auto ticket triage, code review, technical design review, and ticket-to-PR chain deterministic steps, agentic steps, and mandatory human checkpoints:

“You can define pausing, deterministic stop, where the agent will stop. And ask for your approval before it continues.”00:04:32

In her live ticket-to-PR run, a production bug triggers triage, runs root-cause analysis, proposes a fix, and pauses. Replying approve creates the PR; rejecting with a security critique routes the orchestrator back to revise the root-cause step before continuing—and Q&A confirms failed runs can resume straight from the failing step rather than restarting from scratch.

Self-improvement

Retrospectives and scored memory layers

After workflows execute, a background retrospective audits real outcomes—for example measuring that 61% of AI code-review comments over the past week were addressed while 39% went unaddressed—and extracts memory layers injected into subsequent runs. During Q&A, audience members ask whether recalling accumulated memories bloats context or causes drift. Her answer is scoring:

“Not only that, we also scoring those memories. So over time, to make sure there is no drift. We are scoring them, so the next run we run only on top of the memories that have the highest score.”00:07:30

Internal evaluations verify whether a newly added memory actually improved subsequent runs and down-rank ineffective memories automatically.

Governance

Usage limits, permissions, and audit trails

Asked how enterprises keep autonomous workflows from burning through tokens or crossing access boundaries, Berman Gold points to three controls built into the workspace layer: independent permissions separating shared libraries, projects, and MCPs; exportable audit trails (including Prometheus export) tracking every human approval, PR cycle time, and token spend; and hard execution caps:

“So we have the ability to create limits. A of times of number of executions. Per workflow, over a level that you disconnected. Over a project.”00:12:45

Capping runs per workflow, project, day, and month lets teams ship autonomous triggers across GitHub, Jira, and Slack safely:

“if you have a very high governance and very high observability on the flows.”00:12:45

Build your own

Describe your factory in plain words

At the close, she shares a QR-linked builder where teams describe what they want automated in plain English—such as deduplicating Slack alerts, opening tickets, dispatching a fix workflow, and shipping safe patches—and receive a configured workspace with step-by-step setup instructions:

“here you have a place where you can just describe by words. What do you want to have in your software factory? And we will show you how we created just for you.”00:17:17
  • Zero-FDE integration: connect existing repos, skills, models, harnesses, and trackers across on-prem, private cloud, or SaaS without months of services (00:00:19, 00:00:56).
  • Deterministic human gates + scoped agents: constrain each agent’s tools and insert hard approve/reject feedback loops before code turns into PRs (00:02:42, 00:04:32, 00:05:49).
  • Scored memory + hard caps: background retrospectives keep only high-scoring lessons while per-workflow and per-project execution limits bound cost (00:07:30, 00:12:45).
